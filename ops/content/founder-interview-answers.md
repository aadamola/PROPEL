## 3 · Challenges

**1. Biggest challenge**
> Being one person with limited hours. I do every sales call, every site visit, every relationship — and the ceiling on the business is my calendar, not the work. That's why so much of what we've built is automated: not to look clever, but because it's the only way one person delivers at this standard.

**2. Ever wanted to quit**
> `[YOU]` — answer truthfully or skip it. If the honest answer is "it's been weeks, not yet," say that. It's more credible than a manufactured dark night, and "ask me in a year" is a good-humoured deflection that costs nothing.

**3. One mistake, biggest lesson**
> Early on I wanted to buy followers for social proof. It's normal here — everybody does it. I got talked out of it, and I'm glad. The lesson: in real estate, trust *is* the product. The moment someone finds out your proof is fake, every genuine claim you make dies with it. We'd rather be visibly new than quietly dishonest. We say on our own website that we're new and haven't published results yet.

**4. Nigeria's economy**
> It cuts both ways, and specifically. My costs are dollar-denominated — servers, AI, software — and my revenue is in naira, so every exchange-rate move squeezes margin directly. I've built the whole operation to run lean because of it. But the same currency pressure is why diaspora buyers are so active — for someone earning in pounds or dollars, Lagos property is a serious hedge. That's a huge part of the market, and they buy almost entirely through content, because they're deciding from another country before they ever fly in.

## 4 · Differentiation

**1. What makes you different**
> Two things. First, I'm from the industry — I studied Estate Management and I'm on the chartered surveyor track, so I'm not an outsider learning your business on your budget. Second, our AI assistants only say what a client has signed off on. We put a facts sheet in front of every client: units, prices, title status, payment terms. They sign it. That becomes the only thing the assistant is allowed to say, and anything outside it goes to a human. Most automation guesses. Ours refuses to.

**2. Why choose you over competitors**
> Ask any automation vendor one question: *which WhatsApp API do you use?* Most of the cheap ones build on unofficial gateways that break WhatsApp's terms — and Meta bans those numbers. Imagine your business line dying mid-campaign with your buyers on it. We build exclusively on Meta's official business platforms. It costs more to do and it's the difference between a system you can rely on and one you're gambling with.
>
> The other thing: before any assistant of ours speaks to a real buyer, it goes through a test suite — we try to trick it, feed it fake instructions, ask it questions it shouldn't answer. It has to pass every time. I don't know anyone else in this market doing that.

**3. Most proud of**
> The Concierge — our 24/7 AI assistant for property enquiries. Not because it's AI; because of what it refuses to do. It won't invent a price, won't guess at a title, won't project returns. It says "let me get you the exact figure from the team" and hands you to a human. In an industry where people get burned by confident wrong answers, a system that knows the limits of what it knows is the whole product.

**4. Best-selling product**
> I'll be straight — we're weeks old, so I don't have a best-seller yet. What I can tell you is what the market pulled on: our first developer client asked for the AI enquiry system before we'd even pitched it. When customers ask for something you haven't advertised, that's the signal.

**5. What people misunderstand**
> That this is about replacing people. It isn't. The assistant handles the first ten minutes — the questions your team answers forty times a week — and hands over a buyer who's already qualified. Your salespeople stop repeating themselves and start closing. And it never takes payments or gives legal advice. Those are human jobs and they stay human.

## 7 · Advice

**1. Starting with almost nothing**
> Sell before you build. I built a great deal before I had a client, and while the quality shows, the honest lesson is that building is comfortable and selling isn't — so it's easy to hide in the work. Your first customer teaches you more than three months of preparation. Get to them faster than feels ready.

**2. A lesson you wish you'd known earlier**
> That your constraint is rarely money. Mine is hours and relationships. Once you know what you're actually short of, you stop solving the wrong problem.
